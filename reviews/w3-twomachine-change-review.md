# Change review — w3-twomachine: the smallest shared conversation authority for two machines (single voter), a serving step that polls only from the settled cursor and claims before every send, and the live proof plan

Outcome: Rule 63 had only a host-local conversation lease, and the journal runner refuses a multi-machine posture because no shared authority existed (Rule 113). This change adds that authority as a preview module, following design 10's single-voter realization (§2, §16: "one voter with explicitly unavailable failover"). It is a hash-chained, fsynced, append-only log on the authority machine holding: the lease (a monotonic epoch that is never reset, break-before-make expiry measured only by the authority's monotonic clock, and a restart that honours a recorded lease for one full term per §3 Restore); one one-use dispatch-claim per send target, with its outcome; and the settled Telegram intake cursor. Both machines reach it over a bearer-authenticated HTTP face, the authority machine over loopback. A runner-agnostic serving step holds the lease, polls Telegram only from the settled cursor (so a stale poller can confirm only updates already settled), and for each update prepares locally, claims under the current fence, sends once, records the outcome, then settles. A non-owner never polls or sends. An update claimed by another epoch is never resent, even with an unknown outcome (§4). Evidence is in tests/preview/two-machine-serving.test.ts (11 tests): two runners give one reply each and the standby never polls; the owner stops before its claim and the other machine replies once; the owner stops after its claim and the update stays visibly unresolved with no duplicate; an in-flight admitted send completes once; a stale owner's claim, settle and renew are refused; a partition gives one voice; a runner partitioned from the authority never acquires; a lost settle is skipped on redelivery; epochs never reset across a restart; copied fences are refused; a torn tail is cut while a broken chain refuses everything; and a real HTTP round trip works while a wrong secret or conversation is refused. Mutation checks: allowing a send on "already-claimed" fails 3 tests, and weakening the fence check fails the authority test. The journal runner is unchanged, so single-machine behaviour is byte-for-byte. Wiring the runner is the next unit and is gated on an operator decision (see Operator questions).

## Closing block

simplestRobustRoute: The required outcome is that two machines give one reply per message and survive a machine leaving without a duplicate or a lost message. The simplest robust route is the design's own one-voter authority (one append-only log, one process) with a per-send claim, and Telegram's own unconfirmed-update retention as the cross-machine intake queue. The alternatives were consensus, a replicated journal, or copying message content, and each adds machinery this outcome does not need. The fence and the claim prevent the named failure (two runners, a stale or partitioned holder, or a handover all sending twice), which the host-local lease cannot prevent across machines. The settled cursor prevents the second named failure: an owner that fetched but had not handled an update dies, and the platform has already confirmed it. Start guard: acquire before any poll. End-state guard: settle only after claim and outcome. Limit guards: a 30 s term, a 4 KB request body, and keys of at most 200 characters.
