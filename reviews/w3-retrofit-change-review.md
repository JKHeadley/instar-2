# Change review — w3-retrofit: the narrowed retrospective ask is sized from a fixed fraction, so it converges far too slowly to be useful

Subject base: 055486b055ea5beaa00fd6787ab2c80e0bb6db5d
Review state: open

Outcome: w3-retro made an over-cap retrospective pass settle as a named failure and narrow the next ask, but the narrowing is a fixed fraction of the previous budget, and a fixed fraction cannot converge when the estimate is wrong by a multiple. The live record: proof run I-live-20261001-195320 over Justin's preview (status-i-end.json, read 2026-10-02), `retrospective.passes` 9 and 10 both `failed: review output over the cap`, the first supplying 37 cases at 20:45Z and the second — after one halving of the case room — still 23 at 02:45Z, six hours later, and still over. Checks I1a-d, O24a and P50c FAIL meanwhile, so Rules 24, 50 and 51 stay unproven. Cause, named precisely: the ask is budgeted in BYTES (`estimatedAnswerBytes` against `RETRO_ANSWER_BUDGET_BYTES`) and judged in TOKENS (the route refuses a result frame over `SUBSCRIPTION_MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS`, 2048), and nothing ever measured the join. The byte budget converts with `RETRO_ANSWER_BYTES_PER_TOKEN` 2.7, measured from two verbatim live PROSE replies, and the retrospective answer is not prose: the one live measurement of such an answer, `modelCalls.last`'s `retrospective:10` (outcome `uncertain`, `usage.outputTokens` 8192, against a cap of 2048), is more than four times the tokens the byte estimate charges for an ask that size. The gap is in the whole answer's scale, not in the bytes-per-token conversion and not specifically in the per-case term: the case rows are the SMALLER half of the ask (the rows every pass owes are 2595 of the ~3630 bytes a 22-case ask estimates), so halving the CASE room removes the smaller half of a cost that is understated throughout. Fix: a failed pass now records the two numbers that join bytes to tokens — `estimatedAnswerBytes`, what its plan estimated, and `outputTokens`, what its own call usage says the answer really cost — and `measuredAnswerBudget` sizes the next ask from their ratio (`RETRO_ANSWER_BUDGET_BYTES × estimated / (bytesPerToken × produced)`), floored at the rows every pass owes, with the planner's existing floor of one case unchanged. The fallback halving is kept for a pass with nothing to measure, and the smallest budget any pass in the consecutive over-cap tail earns is the one used, so an unmeasured pass can never widen the ask back out past what an older measurement already proved too large. Separately, an over-cap failure now waits the ordinary minimum interval between passes (1 h) rather than the unknown-failure backoff (6 h), while and only while the ask is still shrinking. Evidence: tests/preview/retrospective-measured-narrowing.test.ts (9 tests) replays the real recorded shapes — all 18 over-cap `call-outcome` frames from tests/preview/fixtures/proofroom-summary-cascade-stall-2026-09-30.json at 2229-8192 output tokens, and the live `retrospective:10` count of 8192 — through both the pure sizing and a real journal and worker, and proves both sides of every boundary it adds. tests/preview/retrospective-over-cap.test.ts (7), tests/preview/retrospective.test.ts (33), tests/preview/retrospective-live-shapes.test.ts (2) and tests/preview/journal-migrate.test.ts (5) pass.

## Closing block

simplestRobustRoute: measure the one thing nobody measured, and size from it. The required behavior is that an over-cap pass's next ask fits in one step. The ask is planned in bytes and judged in tokens; the only quantity that joins them for THIS answer is what such an answer really cost, and the provider already records it as the call's usage. So the route is: keep the two numbers with the pass, and divide. That is one ratio inside the function that already computed the budget, plus one integer on each of two rows that are already appended — no new store, format, service, schema or model call, and no change to what the model is asked. The simpler route still available is the one being replaced, a fixed fraction with no measurement, and the named failure it cannot handle is on the live record: an estimate wrong by more than 4× needs four halvings to approach its floor, at six hours each, and may never reach an answerable ask at all (proven for a recorded 3592-token overrun, where every budget the halving can ever reach — including the floor it arrives at after RETRO_ANSWER_NARROW_STEPS failures — is still over the cap, while one measured step fits). Three pieces of added machinery, each against a named failure the simpler version cannot handle: (1) recording the estimate durably rather than recomputing it from the live case population, because a case inspected or aged out since would make the recomputed estimate SMALLER and so the next budget LARGER — silently reproducing the non-convergence being fixed; (2) taking the smallest budget across the consecutive over-cap tail rather than only the newest pass's, because a measured pass followed by an unmeasured one would otherwise hand back more room than the measurement already proved too large (caught by the test, not by reading); (3) the two brakes on the faster retry, because without them a pass sitting at the floor — where the ask cannot change — would be retried hourly forever. Nothing here raises the route's output cap: that means changing `policy.maxTokens`, which changes the invocation-policy digest and so requires a new signed activation record, and this fix does not need it.
80/20: 0 must-fix. Notes, one of them load-bearing. (1) THE HONEST LIMIT, which this unit measures but cannot close: on the live measurement the sizing converges in one step to its FLOOR and the ask still does not fit, because the rows every pass owes — 14 duty rows, 7 gravity-well judgements, the efficiency summary — cost more than the whole 2048-token cap on their own at the rate that answer was written to (2595 estimated bytes × the measured 2.26 tokens per planned byte ≈ 5900 tokens). Across the 18 recorded over-cap counts the boundary is exact and tested: the ten gentlest (2229-3592 tokens) reach an answerable ask after one measured step; the eight largest (3651-8192, the live 8192 among them) are not answerable at ANY number of cases. So for the live preview this unit makes the convergence correct and one-step, and makes the obstacle legible, but it does not by itself make a pass complete there, and I am not claiming it does: checks I1a-d, O24a and P50c will stay FAIL on that journal until the fixed part of the answer is itself bounded. Closing it needs one of two things, both outside this item's scope as briefed: a more compact answer schema for the fixed rows (w3-retro's own 80/20 note (1) already flags this as a separate unit, and it changes the wire format the validator decodes), or a higher route cap (operator-held: a new signed activation record). (2) The faster retry trades spend for convergence speed in one failure class only; the measured sizing would work at the 6 h backoff too, just slower, so note (1) is the reason to keep it rather than widen anything else.
