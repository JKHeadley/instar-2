Outcome: Plan row #548 (observer #179). A deferred task that falls due must run on its own schedule whatever the chat connection is doing; only delivering its result needs Telegram. In the runner (tests/preview/journal-agent.mjs) the scheduled consumer of due work (worker.workObligations, run as the `after` step of the cycle's one ordinary job, sentinelCycle in tests/preview/live-sentinels.ts) depended on the Telegram poll in three ways. (1) Each failed poll waited out its backoff (up to 30 s) in a plain delay loop that offered no work, so a run of failures paused the schedule. (2) Twenty failed polls in a row ended the run ('Telegram polling failed 20 times in a row'), so a connection down for about seven minutes stopped all due work until a relaunch, and the relaunch's carried breaker ended it again on its first failed trial. (3) A drain that failed (for any reply) skipped the cycle's due step. Now the backoff is waited out by waitWorking, which offers the cycle's work job to the lane at most once a second; a sustained UNREACHABLE connection (no answer at all) keeps the poll breaker open at the capped 30 s trial cadence inside the run instead of ending it, while a sustained conflict or a sustained definite refusal from Telegram still ends it as before; and the cycle's due step runs even when its drain failed (the job still fails and backs off). Delivery is unchanged: the trial's reply-only grant has no unsolicited send, so a finished result waits durably in the journal (obligationWork report, awaitingDelivery) and rides the first reply after the connection returns, exactly once (the existing bound/receipt settlement). A launch option `--telegram-cut-file` lets the desk's live check cut a test room runner's polls for a window. The live FAIL that prompted the unit (D-proofroom2-20261004-233340) had a different cause, recorded as a decision below.

## Closing block

simplestRobustRoute: the required outcome is that a due obligation step runs on its slot while the Telegram connection is down, and that its result reaches the operator exactly once when it returns. The simplest robust route through existing mechanisms is what is built: the existing work job and lane are offered during the existing poll backoff (one helper), the existing breaker keeps its backoff and cap but no longer ends the run for an unreachable connection (one predicate), and the existing reply-carried delivery is kept as the only Telegram-dependent step. Not taken: a separate scheduler loop or timer (a second path to the same one-job lane, with its own stop and concurrency handling and no failure it would prevent beyond what the backoff offer prevents), and an outbox with unsolicited sends (outside the trial's reply-only grant, Rule 87). The fault option is the smallest way to make the outage reproducible on a real test room. Start guard: the job is offered only while no stop holds, the runner owns the conversation and the activation matches; end-state guard: the result is settled once on the carrying reply's receipt; limit guards: one job at a time, at most one offer a second, the existing call allowance and capacity fence, the 30 s poll cap.
