Tier rationale: Changes the lost-worker dispatch boundary, durable queue semantics and the shipped host's resource family and pressure gauge; offline tests cannot establish live recovery.
Side effects: A lost worker no longer launches its replacement inline; it becomes a queued revival that passes the drainer on the same tick when the gates allow. With a pressure gauge the first revival waits for three consecutive normal readings (three minutes at the 60-second cadence), as in 1.x. A replacement that dies within 15 seconds with proven no effect is retried after 2 and 4 minutes, then gives up; three consecutive failures across entries open a 30-minute breaker for the conversation. The shipped conversation worker now holds the resource owner's answer family, so it takes the reserve launch and is ordered before maintenance work. Interrupted-launch and uncertain-effect cases settle as before. The conversation record gains revivalQueue and revivalBrakes; an older runner ignores both and its drain accepts only queued rows.
Undo and recovery: Stop the host before reverting. Preserve conversation custody and journal files. A revival-queued row is retained for reconciliation by an older runner; a gave-up or invalidated entry keeps its retained input settled as failed or uncertain and the operator lever requeue reopens a gave-up entry. Completed results and uncertain effects are never replayed. Reverting the host's hold family returns conversation workers to the maintenance family without touching custody.
Multi-machine posture: Machine-local, deliberately. The queue and brakes live in the conversation's host-owned record under the existing conversation ownership fence (tests/preview/conversation-owner.ts), which refuses a second writer on this host and a foreign holder; the 1.x cross-host lockfile and auto-heal are not carried. A queued revival binds parent, owner, placement and scope and is revalidated before launch; a different machine cannot consume it. The ownership gate of worker-recovery.ts (proceed, forward, withhold) is carried with its ports for a future multi-machine owner.
