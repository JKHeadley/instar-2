Outcome: w3-retrofit made the next retrospective ask converge in one measured step but could not make a pass complete on the live journal, and named why: the rows every pass owes cost more than the whole 2048-token output cap on their own. This unit closes that. WHICH PART WAS LONG, from the recorded material: not free prose per case, not restated evidence, and not an unbounded case count — it was the FIXED rows, and specifically the gap between the length the question ASKED FOR and the length the validator ACCEPTED. The question asked for a duty note of 40 characters and `text(row.note, 'duty note', 500)` took 500; asked 30 for a gravity-well note and `redact(row.note.slice(0, 500))` took 500; asked 120 for the efficiency summary and `text(...)`'s default max took 1000. Measured: the fixed part is 2362 bytes at the asked lengths but 12972 at the accepted lengths, 5.5x, which is 4804 tokens at the measured 2.7 bytes per token — over the 2048 cap before a single case row. Repeated keys and enum names, not content, are the majority of even the ASKED figure (1386 bytes of scaffolding against 890 of payload). And the instruction was unachievable as written ("one short sentence (at most 40 characters)"), so the model resolved it toward prose. The live record corroborates that the fixed part dominated: on Justin's preview every retrospective call ever made hit the output cap (`callOutcomeCounts['output-cap']` 11 against `modelCalls.byJudgment.retrospective` 11, status-i-end.json read 2026-10-02), pass 10 among them with 23 cases supplied where pass 9 supplied 37 — cutting the case rows nearly in half did not help. No partial output is stored anywhere for those calls (an over-cap frame carries usage only), so the diagnosis is from the prompt, the validator's own acceptance numbers, the two verbatim live outputs the bytes-per-token ratio is measured from, and the live supplied/eligible counts. Fix: the fixed part is now three fields with no free-text slot but one — `duties`, one character per duty in RETROSPECTIVE_DUTIES order over the enumerated alphabet {n = inspected, nothing found; f = inspected, a finding of that duty is in this answer; u = unavailable}; `wells`, one entry per gravity well in GRAVITY_WELLS order, either 0 or the non-empty ref list that evidences it; and `eff`, the one sentence Rule 51 requires in words, accepted at exactly the length it is asked for. Every per-field length is now one number stated once and used in both the prompt and the validator, and an over-length prose field is CLIPPED to it rather than refusing the pass. All three decode into the same RetroDuty / gravity-well / efficiency records, so every downstream reader (journal-agent's status projection, retrospectiveStatusLine, reviewRecordOf, disciplineSource) and every live proof check (I1a's `efficiency` sentence, I1b's fourteen duty rows and dispositions, I3c's `gravityWellsObserved`, O24a's recurrence duty, P50c's workaround and removable-attention duties) reads exactly what it read before. Measured after: 299 bytes, 111 tokens. Result on the recorded evidence: EVERY one of the 18 recorded over-cap rates, from the gentlest 2229 to the provider's own 8192 ceiling, now reaches an answerable ask after one measured step; before this unit only the ten gentlest did and the eight largest — the live 8192 among them — fitted at no number of cases. I chose the compact schema, not the design-permitted alternative of carrying the duties across successive passes, because the fixed rows DO fit one answer now with large margin (111 tokens of 2048), so spreading them would add machinery and visible omissions to solve a problem that no longer exists (Rule 116).

## Closing block

simplestRobustRoute: The simplest route that bounds the fixed part is to stop asking for prose where no rule requires words, and to make the one number per field hold on both sides. That is what this is: three fields replace twenty-two rows, two of them admit no free text at all, and the third is the single sentence Rule 51 requires. Nothing was added that a simpler route could have done without. Each piece prevents a named failure the simpler route cannot. (a) Aligning asked and accepted alone — the one-line change — does not bound what the MODEL WRITES, only what is stored; the live pass would still spend 8192 tokens and still fail, just with a refusal instead of a cap breach. (b) The positional/enumerated shape is what leaves nowhere for prose; without it the model has a key to expand into and the acceptance alignment only moves the failure. (c) Deriving `RETRO_ANSWER_FIXED_BYTES` by serialising the shape, rather than writing the constant by hand, prevents the exact drift that caused this bug — a hand-maintained estimate that no longer matched the shape it priced. (d) The `f` corroboration is the one thing that keeps the field from being pure ceremony: the disposition axis is fully determined by the plan, so without a falsifiable bit the whole `duties` field would prove only that the model echoed a required constant (Rule 116's "remove ceremony that proves only that other ceremony occurred"). Not built, deliberately: a compact shape for the GRADE rows (the case-driven half), carrying the duties across passes, any change to the route cap, and any extra model call.
80/20: 0 must-fix. Three notes. (1) The case-driven rows are now the whole cost, and a graded decision row is about 410 bytes at live id lengths, so a pass asks about roughly ten decision cases where it asked about five. With 735 cases owed on the live journal that is still many passes; the review now CONVERGES where it previously could not complete at any size, but it does not drain the live backlog quickly. A compact shape for the grade rows is the obvious next unit and this record does not claim it. (2) The compact ANSWERS in the fixture are authored, not recorded, and cannot be otherwise — no model has ever been asked for this shape. What they are checked against is recorded: the malformed classes come from the live `modelJsonShapes` counts, the over-cap frames and uncertain returns from the proof-room journal, the bytes-per-token ratio from two verbatim live outputs, and the pass/eligible counts from the live status file. The fixture says so in its own `source` field. The first live pass is the real proof and it has not run yet. (3) Two assertions in retrospective-measured-narrowing.test.ts recorded facts this unit deliberately reverses — that the fixed rows were most of the ask and exceeded the cap alone. They are now asserted in both directions, with the old 2595-byte figure kept as a literal so the contrast stays in the record rather than quietly disappearing.
