# Change review — w3-twomachine-wire: the journal runner serves one conversation from two machines; every send and model call waits for the other machine's acknowledgement of its record (replicated(1), D1(a)); a successor continues from the newest copy

Subject base: 635a16a1157eb2fc600f4753f40a58f5caf3ec0c
Review state: open
Reviewed content: none
Outcome: D1 was decided as (a), the constitutional default: once a second machine is enrolled a reply needs an acknowledged copy on that machine, and local durability is never chosen when the peer is lost. This change wires the runner to the shared conversation authority of the base unit and adds the smallest replication that meets that demand. With `--machine-posture multi-machine --conversation-authority URL` (plus the shared SecretRef) the runner no longer refuses: it stands by in a loop (no poll, no send) while it receives the owner's journal bytes; it serves once the authority grants it the lease and it holds the newest history; ownerHeld() also requires the shared fence; polls start at the shared settled cursor; every send takes the one dispatch-claim for its target and records its outcome (a definite platform refusal is recorded as refused). Replication: the lease holder ships the journal file's sealed bytes in order; the other machine appends them to a copy, checks the whole-prefix digest, fsyncs, and returns an acknowledgement authenticated with the two machines' shared secret; a send or a model call waits until that acknowledgement covers the journal through its own record. Ordinary work starts only while the peer holds the whole journal, so with the peer away messages are still read but nothing is spent or sent. A machine that takes over installs the newest copy it holds after a full replay verifies it; its own older journal is set aside as a file. Evidence on real runner processes (tests/preview/two-machine-runner.test.ts, two-machine-floors.test.ts; two runners, one real authority process, real HTTP): one reply with both up and the standby never polling; the peer leaves and the next message is read but no call is made and nothing is sent, then exactly one reply after it returns; the owner is killed and the other machine takes over with the earlier exchange in its journal, holds its reply until the first machine is back, and after a hand-back nothing is sent twice and the old journal is kept; a stalled owner that resumes sends nothing and retires; a peer lost mid-turn holds the next model call and the send; a stop on one machine leaves the other unable to spend or send; a machine with no enrolled history never serves. tests/preview/journal-replication.test.ts (9 tests, real journals and files) covers the receiver's refusals, restart, compaction re-send, takeover, the dispatch gate and the HTTP face. Mutation checks: removing the ordinary-work gate, the send gate, the call gate, the acknowledgement requirement or the adoption rule each fails a test. The single-machine launch takes none of the new paths.

## Closing block

simplestRobustRoute: The required outcome is the constitutional default for two machines: one voice, and no reply or paid call unless its record is on both machines. The simplest robust route through existing mechanisms is to copy the one file that already is the durable record (the append-only sealed journal) to the other machine and gate the existing send seam and model-call boundary on its acknowledgement; this proposal is that route. The alternatives were design 06's full per-fact segment replication with per-machine keys, or merging two journals at takeover; both add machinery this outcome does not need. Added machinery and the failure each part prevents: the acknowledgement gate prevents a reply or call whose record dies with one machine; adoption prevents a successor answering without the history; set-aside and the damaged-copy check prevent silent history loss; the one-time seed prevents the wrong machine's journal becoming the history at enrolment; the shared cursor rule prevents Telegram dropping a message only one machine holds; the generation mark prevents a compacted journal being appended to an old copy. Start guard: lease plus verified history before the journal opens. End-state guard: claim and outcome per send, cursor settles only on acknowledgement. Limit guards: 256 KiB chunks, 512 KiB request body, backoff to 15 s on an absent peer, a 10 s bound on the minimal path's wait, the 30 s lease term. Unattended shipped-path evidence: the two-runner process tests complete real takeovers and held replies with no intervention; the live two-machine proof is plan T.
80/20: 0 must-fix. Accepted residue, recorded: failover only toward the authority machine (three voters are a separate design); a turn already in progress when the peer leaves is not repeated if that owner is also restarted before the peer returns; past 100 unacknowledged messages in one peer outage further messages wait at Telegram; the peer acknowledgement uses the shared secret, not per-machine keys
