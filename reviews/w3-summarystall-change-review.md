# Change review — w3-summarystall: a reply is never held because the conversation grew

Subject base: 30bda628294c58d04c895b67460e2ef94af1f084
Review state: open
Reviewed content: none
Outcome: Third failure in about thirty hours on one path (the cascade hand-off, the stuck-summary overflow repairs, and this stall), with three different causes in the rolling summary's span selection and one user-visible harm: the reply is held and every later turn is held behind it. Rather than a fourth selector layer this adds the floor the three incidents share — verbatim history yields LAST. The reply path is unchanged until the ordinary preparation and a forced rolling summary have BOTH failed on bytes; then `preparedWithFloor` re-runs the whole existing selection with a history floor, setting aside the older half of what is still carried per step, by arithmetic with no model in the loop. Each step strictly raises the floor, so it terminates within log2(history) steps and ends with the incoming message alone; the message itself being too long is the one size refusal left (`too-long-input`), and that belongs to the message, not to the conversation's length. Nothing is deleted: the turns stay in the encrypted journal, the packet carries a `historySetAside` block with the exact count missing from it and plain instructions to say so or ask, the packet audit faults a gap that is not disclosed or whose count disagrees, the reply's own durable `reserve` row carries a `kind: 'history'` drop naming the floor, and recall is now offered against the floor when no summary exists (previously verbatim history was the only path to an older turn, so a set-aside turn would have been in no block at all). Also included, from the same lane and the same incident: the narrow span-selection repair — (1) the over-cap ceiling counts only frontiers that still have an attempt, so a spent one stops blocking every later span (a later span carries those turns, because a span is (previous, through]); (2) when no shorter span is offerable the ceiling span takes its remaining attempt; (3) that attempt asks for strictly less, because repeating a byte-identical request is the one retry that cannot succeed; (4) a pass ends after four over-cap attempts and settles the last proven over-cap attempt from its own outcome row.
Affected rules: 2, 9, 15, 42, 60, 95, 96, 110, 116 (15 — a reply held because the agent's own history grew is the agent made unreachable, and the agent is the one who could fix it; 95 — reachability to the user fails open, which is the direction this takes, while change and release integrity are untouched; 96 — full history up to a generous threshold, beyond it summaries: a threshold that could not be exceeded is exactly what was missing, and the rolling summary still runs first and still improves what is kept; 2 — nothing silently lost: the set-aside is counted in the packet, audited, recorded durably with the reply, and the messages stay in the journal, recall and search; 9 — the duty leaves a proof artifact, the `kind: 'history'` drop on the reserve row; 110 — the gap is stated in the same packet rather than papered over; 42 — a refused span stays refused, its two-attempt budget unchanged; 60 — summary spend is bounded per pass and no extra call is spent to answer with less; 116 — arithmetic over the existing selection instead of another retry layer)

## Closing block

simplestRobustRoute: the required outcome is that a growing conversation can never stop the agent answering. The simplest robust route through existing mechanisms is to let the one unbounded block yield like every other block already does — the packet already has a priority order with history at the top, a drop record, a disclosure idiom (`moreUndecidedEdits`, `moreDated`) and a recall path for what history does not carry. This proposal IS that route: one filter in `packetFor`, one disclosure field, one extra recall case, one bounded loop over floors, and two lines in the packet audit. No new store, row kind, judge, model call, format or flag. The added machinery is the loop over floors; the named credible failure it prevents is the live one — a stalled frontier makes every later reply unanswerable, and no cleverer span selection can prevent that because the growth is independent of why the frontier stalled. Its operating cost is preparations only (log2(history) of them, no model calls) and its recovery cost is a revert of one filter.
80/20: 0 must-fix, 3 notes — (1) the floor degrades context quality, honestly and visibly, rather than failing: an answer prepared at the floor may have to say it cannot see an older message, which is strictly better than silence but is not as good as a summary, so the rolling summary remains the thing worth fixing; (2) under severe byte pressure the recall and search blocks can themselves yield after the floor engages, so reachability of a set-aside turn is offered rather than guaranteed in any single packet — the `truncated` flag and the set-aside note are what carry that honestly; (3) halving is a measured engineering default, not a constitutional number.
