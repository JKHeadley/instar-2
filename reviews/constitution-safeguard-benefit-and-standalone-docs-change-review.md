Outcome: Record two operator-approved constitution articles as two new rules. Rule 125, A Safeguard Must Leave a Benefit: every restriction a design adds beyond the constitution's floors names what it protects and what benefit it costs; the design shows that a real benefit survives once all restrictions are in place, as a number where one exists; a design left with no meaningful benefit is unfinished however safe it is; a narrow restriction already covered by a stronger one in the same design is removed unless it adds protection; a practice-only phase states its end when it is introduced; the rule never weakens a constitutional floor, and where the benefit is near zero because of the design's target the remedy is a different target; the ladder Rule 72 requires and the evidence Rule 57 requires are constitutional obligations, not restrictions a design adds, so the rule reaches only what a design adds on top of them. Rule 126, Documents Stand on Their Own: every document written for a decision or for review can be followed by a reader with none of the session's context; it says first, in plain words, what the part of the system does today, what is wrong or wanted and what changes; it defines each term at first use, names things by what they do before any internal name, gives every number its unit and comparison, and never relies on earlier conversation. Rule 125 is in Needs building: no check exists in 2.0 yet, and two parts of it (the restriction list, and the stated end of a practice-only or shadow phase) can be checked for presence once built. Rule 126 is in Held by the mind: whether a document stands on its own is a judgment and no script is owed.
