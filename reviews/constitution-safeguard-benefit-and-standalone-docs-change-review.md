Rule 125, A Safeguard Must Leave a Benefit: every restriction a design adds beyond the constitution's floors names what it protects and what benefit it costs; the design shows that a real benefit survives once all restrictions are in place, as a number where one exists; a design left with no meaningful benefit is unfinished however safe it is; a narrow restriction already covered by a stronger one in the same design is removed unless it adds protection; a practice-only phase states its end when it is introduced; the rule never weakens a constitutional floor, and where the benefit is near zero because of the design's target the remedy is a different target. Rule 126, Documents Stand on Their Own: every document written for a decision or for review can be followed by a reader with none of the session's context; it says first, in plain words, what the part of the system does today, what is wrong or wanted and what changes; it defines each term at first use, names things by what they do before any internal name, gives every number its unit and comparison, and never relies on earlier conversation.
