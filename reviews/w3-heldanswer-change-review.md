Outcome: Fourth defect on the reply-review path in about thirty hours, and the first three were each a different cause with the SAME shape: a condition on that path acquired authority over the WHOLE reply. Plan #111 (a circular unrecorded-blocker read), plan #144 (an undecided check ending in a hold), plan #201 (a fulfillment-only rejection silently inheriting reply-hold authority), and now plan #215: 15 of 294 operator turns since 09-26 ended with ONLY "I need to check that answer before I can send it." and no answer ever followed (5 of 62 on 10-01, on L18s/L19s). This adds the one floor those share instead of a fourth condition: a pre-send gate may withhold only the content it NAMED. The two credential floors keep whole-reply authority, because what they name is the reply's fitness to leave at all; a full-context objection on an untracked deferral or an unevidenced cannot-do claim names CONTENT, its reason already quotes that claim (the review question requires it), so code removes the sentences carrying that claim and sends the rest of the answer. No extra model call, nothing paraphrased or rewritten, nothing added. What was removed, and any named claim no sentence carried, is recorded on the send and counted in `claimScopedWithholds`, so neither a withholding nor a pass is silent. The measured live mechanism is replayed verbatim: both held turns had a completed revision (about 10 s) discarded only because its own review could not return inside the shared 30 s budget, after a first review of 14.8 s and 18.7 s — so on the live build the revision round could never clear a held class, and the notice became the final word by arithmetic, not by judgment.

## Closing block

simplestRobustRoute: the required outcome is that an operator turn never ends with a notice standing in for content no floor objected to. Three routes exist. (1) Release the obligation floor entirely for non-credential classes: one line, and it ends the dead end — but it also deletes build 4's floor, which is the shape of plan #201's regression and would be the fifth defect on this path. (2) Give the revision round a bigger budget or a second attempt: it does not close anything, because the floor would still replace the whole answer whenever the revision fails for any other reason, and it costs model calls. (3) Scope the block to the content the gate named. This proposal IS route 3, built out of mechanisms already present: the reviewer's per-rule findings already carry the quoted claim (the review question requires it and the live runs produce it), the sentence splitter already exists for the Rule 106 link check, the candidate assembly and disclosure re-application are copied from the existing revision path, the record rides the existing `release`/`heldReview` rows, and the counter mirrors the existing `reviewUnavailableReleases`. The added machinery is quote extraction and sentence removal, about 70 lines with no state, no call and no flag; the named credible failure it prevents, which route 1 cannot, is an untracked promise or an unevidenced wall reaching the operator unremoved. Its operating cost is string work on one reply; its recovery cost is a revert of one branch.
80/20: 0 must-fix, 4 notes — (1) removing a sentence can leave the remainder reading slightly abruptly (a dropped "But" or a dangling contrast); honesty over polish was chosen deliberately, and the alternative — rewriting the remainder — would be a second judgment the floor must not make; (2) `CLAIM_MATCH_MIN` is 12 characters, set by the shortest real recorded claim (`I'll summarize then`, 19), and `CLAIM_PREFIX_MIN` is 24 for the truncated-claim path: both are measured engineering defaults, not constitutional numbers, and a reviewer quoting a very short fragment will name a sentence by that fragment; (3) the unlocated-claim release is the one place this change deliberately lets a named objection through — recorded and counted, never silent, and argued above; (4) the layer below is untouched: the live cause of the discarded revisions is a 30 s budget that cannot fit a first review plus a revision plus its review, and that remains true — this change makes it harmless rather than fixing it, which is worth a separate look at whether the revision round is earning its two calls at all.
