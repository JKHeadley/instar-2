## Closing block

simplestRobustRoute: the required outcome is that a reply check gives a verdict when the reviewer is healthy, and stays honestly unavailable when it is not. The simplest robust route is the one taken: change the one constant that measured evidence shows is the binding limit, sized to the measured latency distribution. No retry loop, timer, second checker or new state is added; the existing deadline, call cap, format re-ask and holding notice are reused as they are. Guards: start — the deadline still starts at Jev reservation and survives restart; end-state — past the deadline the outcome is `unavailable` with the budget reason, never a pass; limit — one deadline for the whole loop and the unchanged call cap.
80/20: tests prove both sides: the recorded update 715674330 shape passes at 60 s and is unavailable at 30 s; a 35 s review completes, while a review that needs the whole budget and a re-ask that cannot fit both stay unavailable with the budget reason. The existing reply-check, format-retry, review-unavailable-release, held-reply, journal-summary-crash and reply-latency suites, and every other preview test file that imports the reply-check module (35 files, 407 tests), pass; `node scripts/check-architecture.mjs` passes and `tsc --noEmit` is clean. The expected unavailable rate on the same traffic falls from 17 of 62 distinct subscription checks (27%) to the two not caused by the deadline (one output cap, one 1.8 s failure of unknown cause, update 715674327): about 3%, assuming the seven reviews killed at 30 s finish within 60 s, which the recorded data cannot show because those calls were cut.
