## 13. Operator decisions and honest limits

**Value — decisions have named owners.** The constitution decides boundaries;
the agent chooses technical and experimental settings within them. Genuine
policy gaps below are filed as candidate purpose amendments. These entries
approve no new access, spending, implementation, live blocking review or fleet
release. A recommendation is not an operator decision.

Basis: Purpose's gap rule, authority constraint and “Who decides what.”

**Value — OD-01: how to develop better use of knowledge.**

Decision: Develop contextual assessment inside the existing judgment doorway.
Compare it with a simpler fact-card approach and an independent challenge arm.

Disposition: Accepted direction; implementation and evaluation are still pending.
The choice of algorithm is an agent-owned hypothesis, not dictated uniquely by a
pillar. It can lose to the simpler approach if it does not improve measured results.

Basis: Purpose's wisdom Value and “Who decides what”; rules 57/58/65/95;
accepted R4/R5 recommendation; sections 3/6/8.

**Value — OD-02: using private knowledge without revealing it.**

Decision: Use it privately only when that use is permitted. Share it only when
sharing is separately permitted. If permission is unclear, keep it private and
unused. Consider what the action itself could reveal, not only its wording.

Disposition: Constitutionally settled boundary. Where the consumer cannot enforce
the allowed use, that mode stays inactive. An undefined tolerance for what others
might infer goes to GAP-02; it is not guessed by the model.

Basis: Purpose's trust and authority constraint; rules 57/86/95;
Part 21 §5 and §15 OD-02 as cited in section 1; sections 2/3.

**Value — OD-03: whose feedback can improve the judgment.**

Decision: Verify who gave the feedback and what they are entitled to assess.
Keep evidence from people outside that scope for investigation. Grade the choice,
its reason and the eventual outcome separately, with each affected person visible.

Disposition: Constitutionally settled. Neither operator praise nor model agreement
makes a false premise true or cancels someone else's harm.

Basis: Purpose's wisdom Value; rules 28/29/58/85/108; section 4.

**Value — OD-04: how long to watch an outcome.**

Decision: Begin with an immediate review, then reviews after seven and thirty days.
Watch longer when a known promise or consequence falls later. Reopen the judgment
when supported new evidence changes its basis.

Disposition: Agent-owned timing setting, bounded by preserving commitments and
honest outcome evidence. The timings may change by measurement before a study is
sealed. Later evidence matters because of what it shows, not simply its date.

Basis: Purpose's wisdom and no-silent-loss constraint, “Who decides what”;
rules 24/58/108; sections 4/8.

**Value — OD-05: how a local lesson becomes shared learning.**

Decision: Let local preferences improve local help within the constitution.
Offer eligible lessons to the commons with evidence, limits and a place beneath
a pillar. The commons decides fleet admission, including independent support and
human approval. A contradictory lesson is rejected however many people favor it.

Disposition: Ownership and authority boundary settled. Fleet thresholds and
privacy settings stay with Part 22; there is no Part 23 vote count to approve.

Basis: Purpose's sovereignty, alignment and authority constraint; rules 57/86/108;
accepted local/fleet division; sections 5/12.

**Value — OD-06: how much extra review to add.**

Decision: Begin with bounded assessment inside the current decision and independent
review afterward. Test extra challenge offline or in the background. Ordinary
conversation gains no mandatory semantic blocking reviewer.

Disposition: Agent-owned trial posture bounded by current permission, reachable
help and finite resources. A future live exception must be precisely approved
through its governing owner; this decision supplies none.

Basis: Purpose's authority constraint and “Who decides what”; rules 57/65/86/95;
sections 6/8/10. Current authorized study spend is US$0.

**Value — OD-07: what counts as evidence of improvement.**

Decision: Use the frozen real-case comparison, independent labels, later observation,
separate harm and reason grades, and full failure accounting in the pilot.
Do not claim wisdom from agreeable wording or an average approval score.

Disposition: Agent-owned experimental settings bounded by truthful measurement.
Sample counts, thresholds, resource caps and audit depth are specified in sections
6/8. No human comparison or broader-than-sampled wisdom claim is authorized.

Basis: Purpose's wisdom and evidence constraints, “Who decides what”;
rules 24/58/65/108; sections 6–8.

**Value — OD-08 / P23-GAP-01: when permitted choices hurt different people.**

Question: When every permitted choice causes serious harm to someone, what should
set the priority between the people affected?

Background: Permission tells us which choices are available. It does not always
tell us whose loss should take priority. Care for one person and care for another
can point in different directions even after checking all relevant evidence.

Choices:

- Decide each conflict with the operator when no existing policy resolves it.
- Adopt a defined priority policy for a specific class of conflicts, with limits
  and a way for affected people to challenge its application.

Tradeoff: Case-by-case decisions need attention and may delay help. A standing
policy can reduce delay but risks repeating the same unfair choice if its limits
are wrong. Counting approvals does not resolve the conflict.

Recommendation: Keep unresolved conflicts case-specific while collecting evidence
for a narrowly scoped policy. Do not train an “always share” or “always withhold”
rule from them. Existing immediate consumer defaults and separately permitted
help continue to apply; no policy gap grants permission to exceed a floor.

Disposition: Candidate purpose amendment filed; operator judgment pending.
Candidate text for consideration: “When permitted choices impose competing
supported harms and no existing priority governs, preserve the competing claims
and seek an operator decision within current authority. A reusable priority for
that class requires an explicit scoped policy and later outcome review.”
This text is a proposal, not operative constitutional authority.

Basis: Purpose's wisdom Value and gap rule. The pillars require care and evidence
but do not uniquely rank different people's competing permitted harms;
rules 57/95 retain the current floor. R4 §7 GAP-01; sections 3/5/11.

**Value — OD-09 / P23-GAP-02: what private use may let others infer.**

Question: If using private knowledge could let someone infer it, what limit must
the permission for that use set?

Background: Leaving a name out may not keep a fact private. A meeting time,
invitation or recommendation can reveal it. The source owner should first clarify
what the existing permission covers. This question remains only when that policy
cannot resolve the acceptable risk.

Choices:

- Keep that use unavailable until its possible disclosure fits an explicit boundary.
- Define a narrow, explicit tolerance for a named use and audience, approved by
  those with authority over the source and by the operator for any new policy.

Tradeoff: Keeping the use unavailable can forgo helpful action. A specific tolerance
may permit more help but accepts a stated chance of revealing something. An
accuracy score from the pilot cannot choose or authorize that tolerance.

Recommendation: Retain the first boundary unless a concrete source/use/audience
case justifies proposing the second. Do not invent a universal numerical risk
allowance or treat redaction as permission.

Disposition: Source-policy clarification required first; any unresolved new
acceptable-risk policy is filed here as a candidate purpose amendment, pending
operator judgment. Candidate text for consideration: “A permission for private
use names the audience-visible implications it permits and its enforcement limits.
Where existing policy does not resolve an acceptable inference risk, the mode
remains unavailable until a scoped policy is explicitly authorized.” This proposal
adds no permission and is not an approved amendment.

Basis: Purpose's trust, authority constraint and gap rule; rules 57/86/95;
Part 21 §5's enforceable-private-use boundary, R4 §7 GAP-02; sections 1/3/11.

**Value — honest limits.** This design incorporates completed source and literature
research, an accepted direction, a specified evaluation and filed seams. It has no
executed private real-case pilot, prospective outcome study, measured grader
accuracy, independently witnessed label protection, local wisdom improvement,
fleet admission, human comparison or independent rule 65 convergence. Schemas
cannot prove that a model noticed every implication or explained its actual cause.
No audit chain is infallible, and zero observed violations never proves zero harm.
Owner grants and implementation remain explicit dependencies in section 12.
