**Rule — each forbidden behavior has a positive control and a visible owner result.** Rules
7, 28, 34, 35, 42, 57, 69, 86, 95, 108 and 111; **checks: P21-NF-03–19/21–24**.
These are permanent test specifications for implementation. They use invented people and isolated
stores. They do not invoke real recipients, payment systems, deployments or private histories.
Each scenario must assert actual captured input and attempted effect calls as well as the
returned label. A mock returning `refused` while a hidden sender ran is a failure.

**Rule — fixture truth does not become production identity.** Rules 35, 58, 85 and 89;
**checks: P21-NF-16/17a–j/22**. The synthetic corpus and observed installed-method fixtures
remain versioned public regression inputs. Private Dawn source, private exchanges and unknown
incidents are excluded. A source-unavailable test tests calibrated behavior, not an impossible
requirement to recall what no permitted source supplies. The old baseline's known failure stays
recorded even after its post-migration expected result passes.

**Value — negative tests should expose the user's failure, not only validate a schema.**
The fault locations and paired controls derive from [R1 §§4–8](research/01-instar-1x-memory.md),
[R2 §§3–7](research/02-dawn-grounding.md), and
[R5 §§7–11](research/05-proposals-and-evaluation.md). Their implementation must compare
retained source, candidate, actual input, behavior and effect disposition end to end.
